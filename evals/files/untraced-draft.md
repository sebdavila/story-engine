# Draft to finalize

## Brief
- Audience: the 40 people of a customer support team, all-hands meeting
- Objective: they should keep logging every bug report in the new tracker, even when it feels slower
- Domain: leadership
- Format: spoken, 2 minutes, by the support director
- Length: about 280 words

## Draft

In March, a customer named Ruth Abbey wrote to us three times about the same thing: her invoices were showing the wrong tax rate. Three different agents answered her. Each one apologized, fixed her invoice by hand and closed the ticket. None of them logged it in the tracker. Why would they? It took a minute to fix and ten minutes to log.

In May, Ruth's company did not renew. In the exit call she said: "I kept telling you, and you kept acting like it was the first time."

When our engineers finally saw the problem, it took them one afternoon to find it. The tax table for two states had never been updated. It had affected 312 customers. Ruth was just the one who complained.

Nobody did anything wrong that day. Each of us did the fast, kind thing. But a fix that stays in one inbox is a fix that has to happen again tomorrow.

So here is what I'm asking. When you log a bug, you are not doing paperwork. You are telling the next agent, and the engineer, and the next Ruth, that this already happened. It takes ten minutes. It saved us nothing in March. It would have saved us Ruth.
