# Structure: pitch to a plumbing franchise owner

## Brief
- Audience: Dana Ortiz, owner of a 14-van plumbing franchise, skeptical of software after a failed rollout two years ago
- Objective: agree to a 30-day pilot of our dispatch app on 4 vans
- Domain: sales
- Goal map row: Structure a pitch or proposal
- Format: spoken, in her office, with one printed page
- Length: 4 minutes
- Material: her techs average 3.1 jobs a day; the 4 vans of another franchise in Tucson went to 4.2 jobs a day in 6 weeks; the app needs every tech on a smartphone; setup takes one Saturday; we refund the pilot if jobs per van do not rise

## Beats
1. Shared ground (F224 step 1): the 7 a.m. phone scramble when two emergency calls land and nobody knows which van is closest. 30 s.
2. Destination and benefit (step 2): one more paid job per van per day, without hiring. 20 s.
3. Obstacles (step 3): paper job sheets, dispatcher guessing van locations, techs calling in between jobs. 40 s.
4. Options and our flaws (step 4): hire a second dispatcher, buy the big all-in-one suite, or pilot our dispatch app; admit ours needs every tech on a smartphone and a Saturday of setup, with the workaround (we supply 2 loaner phones and run the Saturday ourselves). 60 s.
5. Make it real (step 5): Luis, a Tucson tech, on a Tuesday: the app routes him from a water heater to a burst pipe 6 minutes away instead of across town. 60 s.
6. Close for both kinds of listener (step 6): growth (4 more paid jobs a day across the 4 vans) and safety (refund if the number does not move). 30 s.
