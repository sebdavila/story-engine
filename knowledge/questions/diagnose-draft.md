# Questions — diagnose-draft

Use to test a finished or half-finished draft, spoken or written. Ask them of the draft, one cluster at a time.

- **Q411** Does the story pass four checks: clear, an important point, 30 to 60 seconds, and a strong emotion or new understanding? Cut it if not. — a short-form gate (Livesay)
- **Q412** Can you finish this sentence: this is the slide or paragraph where they ___? Cut or rework it if you cannot. — purpose of each unit (Edwards)
- **Q413** What do we want the audience to do, think and feel at this slide or paragraph? — Do Think Feel editing (Edwards)
- **Q414** Are you telling their story, not yours? — audience-centered check at every draft (Edwards)
- **Q415** What story did I intend, and what story am I actually telling? — intent versus result (D'Costa)
- **Q416** What is this story really about for you, in a single sentence, and what compels you to tell it? — the One Sentence (Bowles)
- **Q417** Does this detail, character or joke support the One Sentence, or distract, confuse or shield me from being vulnerable? — relevance of each element (Bowles)
- **Q418** Does this flashback move the story and give needed context without breaking flow? — flashback test (Bowles)
- **Q419** What was confusing, slow, superfluous or missing context for fresh ears? — fresh-listener feedback (Bowles)
- **Q420** Whom might this story hurt or stereotype, and what am I accomplishing by telling it? — responsibility check (Bowles)
- **Q421** Am I in command of the emotion or still living the wound? — readiness to tell (Bowles)
- **Q422** Can I tell this without shaking, racing heart, an angry voice or crying; have I moved on; is it over; can I see what it was about? — mishap readiness (Leitman)
- **Q423** Before telling someone else's story: do I have a similar story of my own, and would I accept being its subject? — ethics of borrowed stories (Leitman)
- **Q424** As a witness, did I imagine how I would act, justify what I saw, take sides, enjoy or hate it? — inner monologue for a witness story (Leitman)
- **Q425** After reading it to friends: was it easy to follow, did you root for me, could you keep characters straight, did you relate? — supportive feedback (Leitman)
- **Q426** What did you love about the story, and what worked? — appreciative feedback instead of critique (Simmons)
- **Q427** Would you tell it this way, with these words and gestures, to a friend over dinner? — dinner test (Dicks)
- **Q428** What do I want or need, what is at peril, and would the audience care if I stopped now? — stakes audit (Dicks)
- **Q429** What is the essence of the five-second moment in one sentence, and what is its exact opposite? — finding the beginning (Dicks)
- **Q430** Where are you right now in the scene, and where would a director point the camera? — cinema of the mind (Dicks)
- **Q431** What entertaining, surprising or thought-provoking reason have I given listeners to pay attention? — the hook (Dicks)
- **Q432** Why do I do the strange or habitual things I do? — finding hidden meaning in a frayed ending (Dicks)
- **Q433** Can this external achievement be told as an internal realization or small step anyone relates to? — humanizing a hero story (Dicks)
- **Q434** How does the opening make you feel; does it arouse curiosity or personal memories? — judging an opening (Karia)
- **Q435** Is the conflict strong enough, how can it grow, does it stir emotion and make people wonder what happens next? — conflict strength (Karia)
- **Q436** Who are the characters, what is the conflict and the spark, how does the lead change, and what is the final takeaway? — skeleton check (Karia)
- **Q437** What can I see, hear, feel, smell and taste in this scene? — sensory coverage (Karia; Haven)
- **Q438** Is the character specific and relatable, is there real conflict and thwarted effort, are stakes high, is cause and effect clear, is there an emotional core? — structure bullet-proofing (Biesenbach)
- **Q439** Which details lift and which distract, amplify or mute, and in what order? — editing detail (Biesenbach)
- **Q440** Does this detail support the goal, or is it just interesting or funny? — detail relevance (Biesenbach)
- **Q441** Would an eyewitness recognize this simplified version as fair and truthful, and does it feel like us? — honesty check (Biesenbach)
- **Q442** Does the audience see itself in a relatable character, is entry effortless with a hook and no jargon, is there novelty, does tension keep rising? — RENT self-review (Lazer)
- **Q443** What experience is my muse having reading this, what promise am I making, and did the draft keep it? — muse and promise check (Lazer)
- **Q444** Where do I plateau, hold back, or write brand copy instead of a human voice? — AI-assisted critique lenses (Lazer)
- **Q445** Is my story broken by an and then chain; can I connect points with because, but or therefore? — causality (Barker)
- **Q446** Where does the story take off, where is it slow or meandering, and is it the right story for me? — test-drive questions (James)
- **Q447** Is the main character believable, empathetic and realistically vulnerable; is the setting clear; is the lesson natural or contrived? — story cube review (James)
- **Q448** Is the ending unpredictable, logical and impactful? — pivot check (James)
- **Q449** How does the story twist, and is the payoff the point and the benefit for listeners? — pivot and payoff (James)
- **Q450** Is there a point where I should start later and end earlier; after each scene, what is the feeling, meaning and next want? — boundary and sequel audit (Haven)
- **Q451** Are details specific not general, unique not common, sensory or a fresh simile, showing evidence rather than telling mood? — four detail questions (Haven)
- **Q452** What is the story about, best part, favorite scene and hero trait, what would you change, add or cut by 20 percent, where is the peak, and where are two flat spots? — peer debrief (Haven)
- **Q453** Does the draft have a clear story question, theme, hero appeal, conflict dialogue, jeopardy, struggle, antagonist, causality, climax, a logical yet surprising ending, perspective, voice and tension? — thirteen story-wide checks (Haven)
- **Q454** Are character, goal, conflict and struggle believable for the character and relevant to readers? — real and relevant (Haven)
- **Q455** Did the story grab your interest at once, stay in mind, and make you want to share it? — personal resonance of a story (Haven)
- **Q456** Can you picture it from start to finish, name its five structural elements, understand characters' feelings and goals, tell it in your own words, and fit its conflicts to your audience? — story selection audit (Haven)
- **Q457** Why is the story ordered this way, which moments are essential, and what can you shorten or reorder? — adaptation check (Haven)
- **Q458** Does this literary tale have strong characters, clear problems and active plot beyond lush prose? — tale fitness (Haven)
- **Q459** Is the draft's setup, crisis, pursuit, conflict, climax and aftermath each shown, and do descriptions reveal how the hero was stuck and what it cost others? — case-study scene detail (Hauge)
- **Q460** Does dialogue sound like real speech with natural slang, match the speaker, avoid announcing feelings, and avoid repeating names? — dialogue naturalness (Hauge)
- **Q461** Does the draft circle back to repeat points, and does every scene link to the hero's visible outer goal? — focus and pacing (Hauge)
- **Q462** Are you willing to be afraid and tell it anyway? — courage framing (Hauge)
- **Q463** Who is the hero, what does he want, and who is keeping him from it? — three conflict questions (Maxwell)
- **Q464** Why did I tell it, who was it about, what problem did the hero face, what did the hero learn, what changed? — five-element daily check (Maxwell)
- **Q465** What am I trying to say, and who, what, when, where and why? — core message of a sketch or scene (Cody)
- **Q466** Is this boring? — asked without blame to pull a restless audience back (Simmons)
- **Q467** Is this the right time for a rebuke; can it be heard? — timing of criticism (Simmons)
- **Q468** Does my hero story accidentally cast others as victims or villains, and which archetype am I playing? — hero-story side effects (Simmons)
- **Q469** Which group told the truth, the front of the building or the back? — letting feuding parties see half-truths (Simmons)
- **Q470** When did this most recently occur, where, who was present, and what came next? — moving a speaker from generalities to events (Simmons)
- **Q471** Is it an anecdote, a problem plus extraction, or a full story with voice, setting and moral force? — anecdote versus story (Schwabauer)
- **Q472** Does the opening need nothing ahead of it, does the ending need nothing following it, and is the plot compact enough to be held in memory as a single whole? — wholeness (Aristotle)
- **Q473** If you delete or rearrange an episode, does the whole story break? — necessity of each part (Aristotle)
- **Q474** Did B happen because of A or merely after it? — causality (Aristotle)
- **Q475** Does a summary alone still make listeners shudder and feel pity? — power of plot without staging (Aristotle)
- **Q476** Are the central violent conflicts between family or close friends rather than enemies? — where tragic force lies (Aristotle)
- **Q477** Does the ending resolve from earlier events without supernatural rescue? — earned resolution (Aristotle; Schwabauer)
- **Q478** What is the bare nameless outline before names and episodes? — universal skeleton (Aristotle)
- **Q479** When imagining the scene live, do movements, exits or props contradict each other? — staging consistency (Aristotle)
- **Q480** If an elevated word is swapped for an everyday one, does the line go flat? — diction (Aristotle)
- **Q481** When you see an apparent flaw, is it an artistic error or an outside fact, does it serve a poetic end, who said it to whom and when, and are two statements used in the same sense? — fair testing of an apparent contradiction (Aristotle)
- **Q482** Did you reveal an emotional truth in the opening, and are characters acting rather than reliving backstory? — a strong opening (Cleland)
- **Q483** Did you use filter words such as see, hear, notice, realize or know that tell instead of show? — filter-word check (Cleland)
- **Q484** Does each twist, reversal or danger beat surprise, raise tension and reveal something pertinent? — TRD check (Cleland)
- **Q485** Does the twist feel forced; is it artifice, and should the planted setup or the beat change? — forced reveal repair (Cleland)
- **Q486** What is the most unexpected thing that could happen next? — brainstorm for a twist (Cleland)
- **Q487** Do you have the right identifiable character, and does the explosion fit your objective, when listeners are captivated yet you miss your goal? — diagnosing a story that charms but fails (Hall)
- **Q488** What details in the customer's account engaged your own imagination? — what makes a told story work (Hall)
- **Q489** Is this ending meaningful, inevitable, surprising, true to the genre's promise, emotional and authentic to how I see life? — ending audit (Rubin)
- **Q490** Can I state the central dramatic question in four to eight active words, and does every scene tie to it? — dramatic question relevance (Rubin)
- **Q491** Is the character's expectation fully reasonable, and does every clue hold when readers look backward? — twist fairness (Rubin)
- **Q492** If a defense attorney picked apart the timeline from A to E, where would the change seem hurried or undeserved? — transformation check (Rubin)
- **Q493** What debatable stance does the ending prove in a sentence, and what is the strongest, kindest argument against it? — theme test (Rubin)
- **Q494** What intense feeling do I not fully understand yet, and which rational explanations can I cut to heighten mystery? — transcendence (Rubin)
