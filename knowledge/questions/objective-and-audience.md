# Questions — objective-and-audience

Use before drafting, to fix what the story must do and for whom, and during delivery to read the room.

- **Q225** What do we want to happen after this communication, and what should the audience do, think and feel? — the outcome that replaces what do I want to say (Edwards; Terenzzo; Hall; Livesay)
- **Q226** Who are they, what matters to them, and how can we offer value? — audience value (Edwards)
- **Q227** Where is the audience now on the subject (keen, indifferent, hostile), and what can move them to where we want them? — the distance to travel (Edwards)
- **Q228** What do you want people to do once you stop speaking, and is this getting us closer to the destination? — action-first planning (Maxwell; James)
- **Q229** If the audience forgot everything else, which single takeaway do you want them to keep? — the one message (Karia)
- **Q230** What outcome should result from delivering this story, can the vision be reached on delivery, and would delivery of it count as success? — the vision test (Parikh)
- **Q231** Do you know who they are, what matters to them and their environment; can they recognize themselves in the story? — audience recognition (Parikh)
- **Q232** Which stakeholders can help or hurt the vision, what are their interests given their roles, and what concerns might they hold? — stakeholder map (Parikh)
- **Q233** Who exactly is the audience beyond assumptions, what do they explicitly want and implicitly want (security, belonging, pride), what do they doubt or fear, and what jargon is foreign to them? — deep audience profile (Biesenbach)
- **Q234** What do they share with you, and what are their mood, culture and context? — common ground (Biesenbach)
- **Q235** Who is your primary bullseye audience, who sits in the outer ring influencing them, what measurable action must follow, and what is the single key idea? — audience rings and target (Maestas)
- **Q236** What does each segment need to receive, understand and believe, working backward from the action? — message chain behind an objective (Feigenbaum)
- **Q237** Which of the four stories and objectives is most pressing now, and what point do you want to make? — choosing which story (Hall)
- **Q238** What is the purpose, whom do you want to reach, who should tell it, and why now? — purpose, teller, timing (Forman)
- **Q239** Why are you sharing this story, what does it prove, and how does it relate to the current business situation? — alignment of story and aim (Dolan)
- **Q240** What is your ultimate purpose, the message, and the takeaway? — purpose and takeaway (Dolan)
- **Q241** What should viewers do, where are you leading them, and how should they feel and respond? — end-in-sight goal (Creek)
- **Q242** What does your audience want, when, and in what form? — delivery fit (Creek)
- **Q243** What story, where, and how? — the context trio (Creek)
- **Q244** Who is this story for, what do they think is true, and will it offend those who speak this way or affirm them? — audience assumptions and dialect (James)
- **Q245** Are they ready: do they trust me, are they alert, are they hungry or tired, are they emotionally prepared? — relational, mental, physical, emotional readiness (James)
- **Q246** What do listeners silently ask: why listen, why care, does it make sense, what can I take away? — four silent questions (James)
- **Q247** Who are you, why are you here, and what is in it for you to get my cooperation? — gatekeeper questions listeners ask (Simmons)
- **Q248** What competing story are listeners already telling themselves, and what honorable intention drives their resistance? — resistance as a rival story (Simmons)
- **Q249** What does the action I am asking for mean inside their current story: more pride and less work, or another corporate initiative? — meaning in context (Simmons)
- **Q250** Which matters more to you, being right or influencing people? — priority check (Simmons)
- **Q251** If I cannot find a story that makes the outcome matter to others, does the outcome matter? — validity of the objective (Simmons)
- **Q252** How is your day going? — opening move; follow any sigh or look with what is that look about, then stay silent (Edwards)
- **Q253** What happens next, what do I do, what does my team do, and how will you help us? — implementation questions to answer before the audience asks (Edwards)
- **Q254** What emotional journey must I guide the audience along, and what obstacles must they cross? — emotional route (Edwards)
- **Q255** How do I feel about this meeting or document, how do I think the audience feels, and what are their emotional needs? — emotional briefing (Edwards)
- **Q256** Where have you taken them, what will they think now, and what question will rise next? — audience-first checks while drafting (Edwards; McKinsey)
- **Q257** Do you care about what you will say, is it true, when is the meeting, and what should the audience do after? — presentation strategy (Maxwell)
- **Q258** What is the client's body language saying: posture, hands, feet, arms, gaze, eye and mouth tension, and tone of voice? — nonverbal listening (Maxwell)
- **Q259** Is there a ticking-clock event for buzz, enough knowledge in the audience's mind, an element of taboo or competition, one story element to start with, and a compact, self-identified audience? — viral readiness (Maxwell)
- **Q260** What do you want the audience to know, feel and do? — outcome planning for pitches (Terenzzo; Livesay)
- **Q261** Is the audience a group needing efficient facts, or an entrenched view that must be shifted by felt experience? — whether to use story at all (Gottschall)
- **Q262** What emotion do I need after a key point, and how should people feel? — emotional target per beat (Parikh)
- **Q263** Is this agenda OK with you, do these match your priorities, any questions so far? — delivery checkpoints (Parikh)
- **Q264** How would you respond if you were in that situation? — the you question for large or virtual audiences (Parikh)
- **Q265** Are viewers who are not for you named, and who are your engaged and curious audiences? — audience circles and exclusions (Creek)
- **Q266** How are your audience seeing themselves, what are they good at, what do they want in the short and long run? — audience self-image (Edwards)
- **Q267** How will liking or sharing this reflect on the viewer? — social cost of sharing (Creek)
- **Q268** What story does this audience already know, and which Cialdini lever (consistency, reciprocity, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity) fits? — persuasion lever choice (Butler)
- **Q269** How would you rate the audience reaction from 0 to 6, from no response through agreement, questions, building on the idea, to telling their own story? — scale for big-idea resonance (Lazer)
- **Q270** Do you want to wow a huge crowd or serve a meal your community enjoys? — role clarification (Haven)
- **Q271** Where are the audience seats; is light clear, do sightlines work, can a whisper carry, and does standing loom over small children? — space and sightline audit (Haven)
