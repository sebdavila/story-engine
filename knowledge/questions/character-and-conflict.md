# Questions — character-and-conflict

Use to build or test characters, goals, antagonists, stakes and change in fiction or in a told story with characters.

- **Q495** Who wants what, what will they do to get it, and what will the other side do to stop them? — the core conflict (Cleland)
- **Q496** How do characters perceive the same event differently, and why will the opposing force not yield? — competing views (Cleland)
- **Q497** What do you know now that you wish you had known then, and what did you believe that you now know is false? — a character's turn in hindsight (Cleland)
- **Q498** What does the character want, why, and why again, until history and motives surface? — the why-chain (Haven)
- **Q499** What are the character's goal, flaws, problems and jeopardy; unique traits, hopes and fears; desired ending; view of the antagonist; why like them and why care? — the character game (Haven)
- **Q500** In character, what do you want, what blocks you, whom do you respect or despise, what is the antagonist's deserved fate, and what is your greatest pride and shame? — in-character self-test (Haven)
- **Q501** What drives each character, what do they want, why does it matter to them, how does each scene feel to them, and which unspoken sensory details fill the setting? — character and scene mining (Haven)
- **Q502** What do I want, why now, what happens if I do not get it now, how will I get it, and what must I overcome? — Stanislavski-style playable motive (Simson)
- **Q503** What does the character want, what is on the line, must everyone present be in the scene, and is there any conflict? — a scene's goal (Barker)
- **Q504** Is the goal personal, with a clear finish line, and big enough to create conflict and high stakes? — goal strength (Barker)
- **Q505** What is the worst thing that could happen to the protagonist now short of total failure or death? — raising stakes (Barker)
- **Q506** What must the protagonist learn to do right that they now do wrong? — the lesson of the arc (Barker)
- **Q507** What does my hero stand to lose, which risks would this character most resist, and are risks ordered and stacked? — risk audit (Rubin)
- **Q508** Why must the hero resolve the dramatic question now, and what final pressure makes confirming the choice hardest? — critical decision (Rubin)
- **Q509** Name at least three conflicts for every ring (self, personal, societal, environmental), and check whether the surface, mental, spiritual and core layers all appear. — conflict onion (Rubin)
- **Q510** Is every character, even minor ones, working as hard and smartly as they can toward what they want? — top-of-intelligence (Rubin)
- **Q511** What is each character concealing, why do the stakes count, and how does every scene push the mask toward slipping or locking in place? — masks (Rubin)
- **Q512** Why is the hero's success the worst outcome for the antagonist, and does the antagonist have equal intelligence? — antagonist parity (Rubin)
- **Q513** Where does evil dwell in this story, why face it now, and what might push me to hurt an innocent person? — owning evil (Rubin)
- **Q514** If the hometown, era or nationality changed, would the characters stay intact or collapse? — setting test (Rubin)
- **Q515** What does the antagonist do on the other side of the chessboard, knowing the hero but misreading the strategy? — antagonist view (Schwabauer)
- **Q516** What is the external goal, and what internal flaw or private lie blocks it? — outer goal and inner block (Schwabauer)
- **Q517** What price has the protagonist paid to prove the goal is worth caring about? — cost (Schwabauer)
- **Q518** What changes between beginning and end, and did the middle's tension cause it? — causal arc (Schwabauer)
- **Q519** Who is conspicuously absent from a key scene, and what arc does that absence imply? — significant absence (Schwabauer)
- **Q520** Who is this person from their own view, and who must they become to make this right? — self-concept (Storr)
- **Q521** What does A think B thinks of A? — social perception in a scene (Storr)
- **Q522** Have you reported what they did, or revealed who they are and what sets them apart from everyone else? — character through specifics (Storr)
- **Q523** When unexpected change strikes, what is the character's automatic, flawed tactic to control the world? — the go-to tactic (Storr)
- **Q524** What belief has the character made sacred to the point of irrationality? Finish: I am only safe when I ___; people only love me when I ___; nobody must know ___; the terrible thing about others is ___. — sacred flaw (Storr)
- **Q525** Why this unexpected change for this character, and what chain reaction will break who they are? — fit of the inciting event (Storr)
- **Q526** How does the flaw give status, superiority, closeness and joy, and what do they dread losing if they abandon it? — what the flaw pays (Storr)
- **Q527** At the ignition point, does the protagonist come across as fairly selfless and low in status, facing far mightier Goliaths? — tribal sympathy (Storr)
- **Q528** What does the protagonist consciously want, subconsciously need, which event embodies healing, and what backfires if they get the want without the need? — want versus need (Storr)
- **Q529** Why is this the final straw, why is today the worst day, why must they act within hours or days, why secret, whom do they fear, what hangs in the balance? — pressure at the ignition (Storr)
- **Q530** What four stages mark the character's development, what reversals pull them back, and which antagonists magnify the flaw while allies disprove the origin wound? — development ladder (Storr)
- **Q531** Has the protagonist changed goals logically, or confused mini-goals with overall goals, or written want versus need? — goal drift (D'Costa)
- **Q532** Does the protagonist chase several overall goals that are only thematically linked? — goal unity (D'Costa)
- **Q533** Would the backstory cause major psychological fallout, and does it hint at a more intriguing frontstory? — backstory weight (D'Costa)
- **Q534** Do all six hero indicators point to one character, and do the title, query and cover copy match? — who is the hero (D'Costa)
- **Q535** What happens to stakes and credibility if the protagonist or antagonist is much older or another gender? — variation test (D'Costa)
- **Q536** What is the weight of each romance subplot, and would removing one leave holes or go unnoticed? — subplot load (D'Costa)
- **Q537** Which minor character, unrelated complication, mirrored emotion or real procedure can become a subplot tied to the ending? — subplot source (Cleland)
- **Q538** Does the ending match the protagonist's definition of happiness, and do loose subplots betray ambivalence? — ending fit (D'Costa)
- **Q539** Does this character have a motivation that helps or hinders the story goal; what are the role, take on the Big Idea, relation to adversity, goals before and after the inciting event, need and main trait? — character necessity (Emunds)
- **Q540** What situations in real life make observers desperate to interfere? — tension seeds (Emunds)
- **Q541** What does each speaker want, fear, and which indirect tactics will they use to push an agenda? — subtext before dialogue (Emunds)
- **Q542** What if the protagonist had a different profession, the story took place two hundred years earlier, or the genre changed? — variation to find the core (Barker)
- **Q543** Whose story is it, and who carries the emotional arc even if another drives the plot? — owner of the story (Simson)
- **Q544** What happens in the story before what it is about? — causal action first (Simson)
- **Q545** Which media, character moral level and narrative mode fit this story? — basic choices (Aristotle)
- **Q546** Do speeches and actions show a deliberate moral choice, fit the role, feel relatable and stay consistent? — character coherence (Aristotle)
- **Q547** Are the antagonists flat appetite machines for an out-group, or round humans shaped by moral luck; could this be told without villains? — caricature audit (Gottschall)
- **Q548** Do the predicament, moral conflict and ending flow from character rather than late coincidence? — grammar of story (Gottschall)
- **Q549** Which ideals are paired at the ending, are they truly compatible, or is the story hiding a comforting lie? — ending honesty (Schwabauer)
- **Q550** How does this genre's core need, life-value spectrum, core emotion and core event unite; and which expectations does the audience bring? — four core framework (Coyne)
- **Q551** Genre check, Action: how do I overcome powerful external forces that mean to kill innocents and me? — core question of action (Coyne)
- **Q552** Genre check, War: how does the group survive while keeping its humanity, and is the cause worth dying for? — core question of war (Coyne)
- **Q553** Genre check, Horror: how do we protect our lives and beliefs when victimized by a manifestation of our fears? — core question of horror (Coyne)
- **Q554** Genre check, Crime: how do we expose those who break society's codes, learn motives, and decide fair punishment? — core question of crime (Coyne)
- **Q555** Genre check, Thriller: how do we confront incomprehensible forces of evil in everyday life? — core question of thriller (Coyne)
- **Q556** Genre check, Western: is the self-reliant individual a danger to order or needed to defend the powerless? — core question of western (Coyne)
- **Q557** Genre check, Love: how do we navigate love's minefield to find a mate, avoid heartbreak and keep a bond? — core question of love (Coyne)
- **Q558** Genre check, Performance: will we do what it takes to express our gifts despite obstacles? — core question of performance (Coyne)
- **Q559** Genre check, Society: what do we do in the face of tyranny, resist or comply? — core question of society (Coyne)
- **Q560** Genre check, Status: will the protagonist chase society's definition of success or discover their own? — core question of status (Coyne)
- **Q561** Genre check, Morality: given selfishness or altruism, will we use our gifts in service of darkness or light? — core question of morality (Coyne)
- **Q562** Genre check, Worldview: how do we tackle problems we don't yet grasp and cope with events that our beliefs can't absorb? — core question of worldview (Coyne)
- **Q563** What genre does my logline, cover sketch and trailer project; entering with only Act One, what genre would audiences conclude? — genre impression (D'Costa)
- **Q564** Looking through a pile of bestselling titles in my genre, how do plots open, develop and close, what do roughly 85 percent have in common, and which signature scenes do reviewers adore or hate? — genre scan (D'Costa)
- **Q565** Do my tonal choices shrink the audience needlessly, or would softening erase what makes the story unique? — tone versus reach (D'Costa)
- **Q566** Who is my readership, which genres do they read, which masterworks will they measure this against, and what is my niche? — audience and niche (Emunds)
- **Q567** Which POV serves the genre, theme or perspective I want to highlight, what do readers prefer, and how deeply should they be immersed? — POV choice (Emunds)
- **Q568** What if I reverse one premise of a familiar Big Idea, such as all castaway stories are hope stories, so what if there is none? — big idea generation (Emunds)
- **Q569** Who accesses which power, how does it manifest, what limits and costs apply, can it be stolen, which power beats which, and who regulates it? — power system of a world (Emunds)
- **Q570** Which theme grows out of the protagonist's problem, weighty choices, contrast with the antagonist, ancillary conflicts and resolution? — theme emerging from story (D'Costa)
- **Q571** What is the novel about, why do I care, and what must the protagonist learn? — the so-what of a long work (Barker)
