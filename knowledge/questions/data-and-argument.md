# Questions — data-and-argument

Use for data stories, business cases, slide decks and structured arguments: framing the problem, testing logic, and choosing what to show.

- **Q371** Who is the audience, what information do they want, and is all the data relevant to those questions? — pruning raw tables into information (Lo Duca)
- **Q372** Why does this situation happen? — external causes that turn information into knowledge (Lo Duca)
- **Q373** Will the hero get what they want? — dramatic tension around the chart's subject (Lo Duca)
- **Q374** What can we change, which opportunities are open to us, what strengths does our program bring, and which scenarios can we sketch? — wisdom and calls to action (Lo Duca)
- **Q375** Does the model need domain-specific information; should you use the base model, fine-tuning or retrieval? — choice of AI setup (Lo Duca)
- **Q376** Is this generated output truly anchored to the chosen ethical framework? — checking AI next steps before publishing (Lo Duca)
- **Q377** Does the AI-generated content respect human rights, society, diversity and inclusiveness, and the environment? — four-point ethics audit of an AI asset (Lo Duca)
- **Q378** Whom does this information describe and how did they end up in the dataset; what does it reveal about them? — people behind the rows (Feigenbaum)
- **Q379** Where and when does the data apply, what events overlapped, and why should the audience care? — place, time, purpose (Feigenbaum)
- **Q380** How was the data gathered and refined and by whom, what analyses were run and why, and which patterns and anomalies fit its backstory? — data backstory (Feigenbaum)
- **Q381** Which perspectives are present or missing in our team and sample at each stage, and how does that limit what we can claim? — standpoint and bias (Feigenbaum)
- **Q382** Who is counting the uncounted, and who or what is missing from this chart, timeline or map? — absence check (Feigenbaum)
- **Q383** What does this icon show by denotation, by connotation and as myth; does a caption anchor its meaning? — semiotic audit of a visual (Feigenbaum)
- **Q384** Which three pictures show what you want changed, what should stay, what represents the host place, and what represents home? — photovoice prompts (Feigenbaum)
- **Q385** What impact does the problem have, and is addressing it within the decision-maker's authority? — moving up the issue tree (McKinsey)
- **Q386** Is each branch of the issue tree a full-sentence question, mainly how can we or why is? — issue-tree wording (McKinsey)
- **Q387** What is going on and how did we get here? — the situation act (McKinsey)
- **Q388** What critical assumptions did you make, how certain are you, and what are the upside factors and downside risks? — credibility in the complication (McKinsey)
- **Q389** How can the organization get out of this mess, and are there more radical actions to turn lower losses into profit? — the resolution act (McKinsey)
- **Q390** How should the organization respond to the projected trend, and what can turn it around? — implications (McKinsey)
- **Q391** What natural question does this slide's title trigger, and does the next slide's title answer it? — owning the flow (McKinsey)
- **Q392** Does the slide body prove the claim in its title, and can I cover it in three minutes? — slide proof and pacing (McKinsey)
- **Q393** Will slides speed this meeting toward yes, or hinder dialogue? — slide utility gate (McKinsey)
- **Q394** Do we know enough about the problem to build a story that answers the real business needs? — problem framing (Parikh)
- **Q395** These are the areas we considered; do you agree, is anything missing, which matter most? — co-creation checkpoint (Parikh)
- **Q396** Is the starting point an observable fact or a point of agreement you already share? — premise of an argument (Parikh)
- **Q397** Has all relevant information been included, and can each premise be substantiated? — deductive safety tests (Parikh)
- **Q398** Are three to five good and different reasons supplied, and aligned with the audience's priorities and decision criteria? — inductive criteria (Parikh)
- **Q399** Should the conclusion or call to action come first or last? — ordering the argument (Parikh)
- **Q400** If only titles and subtitles remained, could a reader follow the story? — headline test (Parikh)
- **Q401** Which examples trigger dopamine, oxytocin or endorphin, and will you use or avoid cortisol and adrenaline? — emotional chemistry of examples (Parikh)
- **Q402** What does this data tell us, what story does it carry, and how do I get it across? — chart message (Parikh)
- **Q403** Can viewers decode the key message in seven to ten seconds, and what would they take from the title alone? — chart legibility (Parikh)
- **Q404** Do I rationalize with facts or dramatize to shift a view; does persuasive intent show and trigger resistance? — telling versus showing (Gottschall)
- **Q405** Does the framing evoke activating emotions such as anger or awe, or deactivating despair? — mobilizing power (Gottschall)
- **Q406** Am I building from falsifiable evidence, or does an inherited master narrative choose my facts? — confirmation bias self-audit (Gottschall)
- **Q407** If this account reads as a defense brief or an indictment, what inconvenient facts were cut? — distortion in nonfiction (Gottschall)
- **Q408** Is this surprising or new, does the data shock people, and what does not fit? — novelty in data (James)
- **Q409** What story does your data book or deck tell, who benefits and who stands in the way? — stakes of the data story (Sandler)
- **Q410** Is the point stated so that a reader can name the focal message and strip dilutive detail? — what do you see there test (Kamath)
