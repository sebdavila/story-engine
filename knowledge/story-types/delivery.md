# Story types — delivery (S092–S110)

## S092 · Literary tale told verbatim
**Purpose:** Deliver an authored story or narrative poem whose exact wording is part of the art. **Teller:** A performer who reads or memorizes the text.
**Choose when:** The piece is a literary tale, a story-length poem, a ballad or a book excerpt, and paraphrasing would damage it.
**Shape:** No F needed; the author's structure stays intact. Decide up front between reading aloud and memorizing word for word, then deliver the full emotional inflection rather than a flat recitation.
**Sources:** Haven

## S093 · Jump tale
**Purpose:** Startle the audience for fun. 
**Choose when:** You want a ghost or suspense story that ends in a sudden startle.
**Shape:** Follow F086 (expectation, tension, revelation): keep the voice hushed, let tension climb through whispers, then drop a sudden loud shout on the final beat.
**Sources:** Haven

## S094 · Participation story
**Purpose:** Make listeners co-performers. **Audience:** Seated groups, or volunteers brought on stage.
**Choose when:** You want listeners to answer on refrains or to act in the story, using a simple multi-character plot.
**Shape:** Two options. (1) Seated: build the tale around a repeated refrain and teach the audience a sound or gesture to answer it each time it returns. (2) Cast of thousands: use a simple plot with many small roles, call volunteers up, and let them improvise the parts. For degrees of involvement see F184.
**Sources:** Haven

## S095 · Honoring stories: toast and eulogy
**Purpose:** Celebrate or remember another person. **Audience:** The room at a celebration, ribbon-cutting or memorial. The honoree, not the teller, is the hero.
**Choose when:** You must speak about someone else in the room or for someone who is gone.
**Shape:**
- Toast: one to three minutes. Pick one scene that shows an affectionate trait of the person or team, tell it vividly, and finish on a clean exit line such as "Cheers!". No personal transformation arc is needed. See F223 for the one-trait, one-story build.
- Eulogy: anchor the grief in specific, idiosyncratic details that bring the person back to life in the room. Optionally organize everything around a single theme word, for example "grateful". (Bowles)
**Sources:** Bowles

## S096 · Everyday transition tales for children
**Purpose:** Move a resistant child through a chore or change of activity without nagging. **Audience:** A resistant child. **Teller:** An improviser.
**Choose when:** The child resists walking, drinking, brushing hair, stopping play, or is overwhelmed in a noisy shop.
**Shape:** No formal F; improvise a tiny quest in a few lines. For physical transitions, invite the child onto a magic carpet or into a mini-mission, such as ferrying a toy across a river on the way to the bath. For errands, recast the shop as a treasure hunt, call the list items treasures, and put the child in charge of the list.
**Sources:** Miller

## S097 · Commencement address
**Purpose:** Send graduates off with one original idea. **Audience:** The graduates only, never the parents. 
**Choose when:** You are giving a graduation speech.
**Shape:** Build around one original, granular takeaway. Leave out self-praise, weather remarks, dictionary quotes and predictions about the future.
**Sources:** Dicks

## S098 · Modular stump-speech obstacle stories
**Purpose:** Fill a long engagement with repeatable career stories. 
**Choose when:** You have a long speaking engagement and want repeatable career-turning-point modules.
**Shape:** Prepare several five-minute modules, each with four beats: the goal you wanted, the goal denied, the obstacle you overcame, and the afterglow. Use F006 to map goal, obstacle and outcome in each module, then string the modules together for the engagement.
**Sources:** James

## S099 · Bad-news presentation
**Purpose:** Present unfavourable findings and move the room toward solutions. **Audience:** Senior stakeholders reading red indicators or audit results. **Teller:** A presenter acting as a neutral facilitator.
**Choose when:** The findings are negative and there is a risk of finger-pointing.
**Shape:** Tell it as a high-tension story but keep the tone neutral so blame does not become the topic, then pivot the discussion to what happens next. For the sequence of situation, complication and resolution use F064; for owning failure openly see F329.
**Sources:** Parikh

## S100 · Entertainment and Escapism
**Purpose:** Give pleasure, wonder or a short mental break. 
**Choose when:** The main goal is emotional immersion, escapism, wonder or pleasure.
**Shape:** Make funny, dramatic, surprising or pop-culture content, or a game, whose value is the experience itself. Vaynerchuk applies this to social posts and games. Use F086 for surprise and F207 for where to place humor. Pair with the other content types in S101, S103 and S104.
**Sources:** Paulsen; Vaynerchuk

## S101 · Engineered Shareable Short-Form
**Purpose:** Get a short piece passed on, remixed and answered. **Audience:** Social media communities. 
**Choose when:** You want sharing, remixing and community response, and can engineer for it.
**Shape:** Build a short hook on purpose so it invites sharing, remixing and replies. Creek separates accidental captures of a freak or funny moment from videos engineered on his five planks to trigger sharing. Test the hook against F370 (relatability, novelty, tension, ease).
**Sources:** Paulsen; Creek

## S102 · Urban Legend
**Purpose:** Spread participatory folklore virally across communities. **Audience:** Communities.
**Choose when:** You are analysing how a participatory tale spreads, or warning how unchecked belief in a story can lead to real-world harm.
**Shape:** A participatory tale moves virally through a community, and unchecked belief in it can spill into real-world harm.
**Sources:** Paulsen

## S103 · Human Connection Content
**Purpose:** Satisfy people's wish to connect with other people. 
**Choose when:** You want to satisfy people's desire to connect with other people.
**Shape:** Post conversational updates, replies and relatable everyday moments.
**Sources:** Vaynerchuk

## S104 · Utility and Editorial Content
**Purpose:** Help customers solve a problem and own the audience relationship. **Audience:** Customers and prospects. **Teller:** A brand.
**Choose when:** People have practical questions, or you want to own audience relationships directly.
**Shape:** Two scales. Short: how-to videos, infographics, reviews and practical advice. Long: editorial pieces such as videos, reviews, publications and guides, produced openly by the brand as a media company and run alongside daily micro-content. For data-driven versions see F160.
**Sources:** Vaynerchuk

## S105 · Observational stand-up
**Purpose:** Spark instant recognition and create inside jokes. 
**Choose when:** You want instant recognition around shared annoyances or workplace quirks.
**Shape:** Share a personal feeling or a fresh take on a shared annoyance or workplace quirk. See F207 for placement.
**Sources:** Cody

## S106 · Anecdotal stand-up
**Purpose:** Humanize a leader and win emotional investment in a pitch. **Teller:** The leader, as the underdog.
**Choose when:** You want to humanize yourself as a leader and win emotional investment in a pitch.
**Shape:** Tell a true underdog, fish-out-of-water or embarrassing story about yourself, with a punch line in every beat rather than only at the end. Link to the pitch with F035 or F235.
**Sources:** Cody

## S107 · Put-down humour
**Purpose:** Puncture hierarchy or sharpen a bold brand voice. **Audience:** A room where the target and everyone else are in on the joke.
**Choose when:** You want to puncture hierarchy, or give a brand a bold voice on social media.
**Shape:** Rib someone with status in good nature, punching up. Use it only when both the target and the room are in on the joke.
**Sources:** Cody

## S108 · Short-form improv games
**Purpose:** Train single skills such as listening and building. **Audience:** Teams.
**Choose when:** The team needs to practise one skill in isolation.
**Shape:** Run quick rule-bound games such as Yes and..., One-Word Story, First Letter Last Letter, Excuses or Cocktail Party, one skill per game. For a full workshop sequence see F220.
**Sources:** Cody

## S109 · Long-form improv scenes
**Purpose:** Co-author messages or pivot live when the script collapses. **Audience:** Teams.
**Choose when:** The team must create or rescue a message in the moment.
**Shape:** Start from one central idea and let an unscripted scene grow through beginning, complication and resolution. See F277 for improv-led co-creation.
**Sources:** Cody

## S110 · Three-thread improv pieces
**Purpose:** Model interconnected, multi-thread storytelling. **Audience:** Teams.
**Choose when:** You want to inspire interconnected, multi-thread corporate storytelling.
**Shape:** Take a single-word prompt, improvise three acts, and weave three storylines together. Compare with S109.
**Sources:** Cody
