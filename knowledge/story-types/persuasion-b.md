# Story types — persuasion (S126–S140)

## S126 · Cautionary story behind a rule or refusal
**Purpose:** Let listeners feel why a rule or a no exists instead of merely complying. **Audience:** People about to be refused, or tired of warnings about safety or IT security. **Teller:** The rule-maker, gatekeeper or leader.
**Choose when:** You must say no, or a policy is being ignored because of warning fatigue.
**Shape:** Tell the human consequences of past harm first, then deliver the refusal or the rule (Simmons); show the human stakes behind the policy so people move from compliance to buy-in (Biesenbach). Matching a story to refusals and sensitive warnings: F278. Absorption with an implicit message: F265.
**Sources:** Simmons; Biesenbach

## S127 · Mistake or failure lesson
**Purpose:** Share a real mistake or disaster and what it taught, so others avoid it and the teller gains trust through humility. **Audience:** Colleagues, prospects, design teams. **Teller:** The person who made the mistake, or a client's cautionary case.
**Choose when:** You want rapport without lecturing, to pass on a behavioral standard, or to show the cost of inaction.
**Shape:** Regret, a disaster that happened to you, or a mistake you caused, with the lesson (Callahan; Dolan); design failures told openly (Sandler). Hauge's version shows you or a client choosing inaction or a rival's weaker approach and ending worse off, usually just before a success story from F238. Check the event is over with F143; owning failures: F329.
**Sources:** Callahan; Dolan; Hauge; Sandler

## S128 · Triumph and inspiration story
**Purpose:** Model success and spark imitation, with humility. **Audience:** Teams, clients. **Teller:** The leader, or a colleague describing others.
**Choose when:** You need credibility in an area, confidence, or pride in ordinary colleagues.
**Shape:** Personal achievement, the courage to try, helping a team or a community succeed (Dolan); concrete instances of colleagues going beyond expectations (Callahan). Avoid boasting: F259.
**Sources:** Dolan; Callahan

## S129 · Tension story
**Purpose:** Build credibility around values by showing them tested. **Audience:** Teams facing tough choices. **Teller:** The leader.
**Choose when:** You want to talk about personal or company values honestly.
**Shape:** An inner struggle or external conflict where values, loyalties or obligations clashed, including a time you chose badly (Dolan). Close with what you do now. Arc: F060.
**Sources:** Dolan

## S130 · Transition story
**Purpose:** Humanize a leader, set a personal brand and guide others through change. **Audience:** Teams in organizational change. **Teller:** The leader.
**Choose when:** Introducing yourself or guiding people through a shift.
**Shape:** A major life or career turning point, forced on you, chosen between options or started by you, centered on your fears, anxieties and excitement (Dolan). Career structure: F338; change arc: F021.
**Sources:** Dolan

## S131 · Rise-setback-recovery business stories
**Purpose:** Show persistence through the rise-fall-rise shape. **Audience:** Press, interviewers, investors, boards, LinkedIn readers. **Teller:** Founder, leader or professional.
**Choose when:** You have early traction, a major setback and a comeback. Pick the variant: industry challenger, a counterintuitive method that gains traction, meets incumbent pushback and persists (press, job interviews, LinkedIn); resilient leader, a strong team hits post-honeymoon friction and fixes communication (interviews, boards); pivot, a promising product fails and a pivot finds fit (press, investors); career pivot, success in one field, a realization it is not your calling, a struggle, then thriving elsewhere (Lazer).
**Shape:** Plot the fortune line with F045 (Boy Meets Girl shape). All variants follow the rise, setback and recovery line.
**Sources:** Lazer

## S132 · Cinderella business stories
**Purpose:** Tell a humble start, a break, a near-fatal crash and lasting triumph. **Audience:** PR, investors, interviewers, brand audiences. **Teller:** Founder, employee, brand or overlooked professional.
**Choose when:** The person or brand started low. Variants: rags-to-riches founder, from poverty or outsider status through a mentor or break and a near-fatal crisis to scale (PR, LinkedIn, investors); career transformation, escape a dead-end role through a mentor's open door and recover from a crisis (interviews); brand renaissance, an outdated brand admits flaws, catches a rebrand break, survives backlash and wins a new generation (case studies, PR); underestimated to industry leader, overlooked, trained, publicly failed or fired, then an icon (Lazer).
**Shape:** Follow the Cinderella curve in F045.
**Sources:** Lazer

## S133 · Change and strategy story
**Purpose:** Help people accept a transition and carry out the next stage. **Audience:** Employees, stakeholders, shareholders. **Teller:** Leaders.
**Choose when:** Explaining a strategic shift, a hard decision or a new idea.
**Shape:** Core F239: before, the change, now, next. Guptara's X Story runs past, pivot, present priority, pitfalls and potential, with anecdotes in each part. Edwards (voyage and return) includes slow recognition, failed early experiments, a tipping-point catalyst and internal advocates, returning with new behaviors. Bowles: world before, your aha moment despite skeptics, how it changes everything, where things stand. Forman: a data-backed future story in chapters, F266. For the pitch version, F268.
**Sources:** Edwards; Guptara; Bowles; Forman

## S134 · Vision story
**Purpose:** Make a shared future concrete and sensory so people rally and present sacrifices feel worth it. **Audience:** Customers, employees, investors. **Teller:** Leader or company.
**Choose when:** Rallying around a long-term purpose beyond daily transactions.
**Shape:** State the lofty long-term future the company is building (Butler); weave present frustrations into a sensory picture of the shared future (Simmons). Callahan: find a corner of the firm or industry where the desired future already happens, tell what they did, and end by asking people to imagine it everywhere; if none exists, open with an openly signposted scenario, walk through one specific customer interaction, then imagine it across the network. Salmon: future-scenario stories about what could happen.
**Sources:** Butler; Callahan; Salmon; Simmons

## S135 · What is versus what could be
**Purpose:** Mobilize people for change. **Audience:** Audiences for activism or change. **Teller:** Advocate or presenter.
**Choose when:** You want to build tension between today and a better future.
**Shape:** Alternate the present reality and the better possibility, back and forth, in sparkline form (Rodriguez). Compare with S134.
**Sources:** Rodriguez

## S136 · Innovation story
**Purpose:** Show persistence against resistance to a new idea. **Audience:** Teams working on ideas. **Teller:** Leader.
**Choose when:** Bureaucracy kills ideas, or you need a cross-industry analogy.
**Shape:** Tell of persistence overcoming resistance, bureaucracy killing an idea, or an analogy from another industry (Callahan). Analogy: F050.
**Sources:** Callahan

## S137 · Springboard story
**Purpose:** Let listeners picture a new path and act. **Audience:** Groups to be moved. **Teller:** Leader.
**Choose when:** You want collective momentum toward change.
**Shape:** A short true story from the past that illuminates the future path (Salmon). Stop before preaching: F279.
**Sources:** Salmon

## S138 · Decision-influence story
**Purpose:** Update the mental catalogue behind intuitive judgments. **Audience:** Decision-makers. **Teller:** Advisor or seller.
**Choose when:** Deciders rely on gut feel.
**Shape:** Give them new, vivid, emotionally resonant experiences (Callahan). Emotion as input: F261; story before argument: F255.
**Sources:** Callahan

## S139 · Leadership stories
**Purpose:** Rally a group around a shared problem and a change of direction. **Audience:** Organization. **Teller:** Leaders.
**Choose when:** The group's transformation is itself the point.
**Shape:** Make the transformation the central story (Maxwell); model: F060. Strategy: F239.
**Sources:** Maxwell

## S140 · Peer-insight story borrowed from famous figures
**Purpose:** Draw strategic lessons from famous or historical people. **Audience:** Teams. **Teller:** Leader.
**Choose when:** No peer story exists; it is less relatable and durable than stories about peers, so prefer S128.
**Shape:** Tell the figure's story, then state the lesson (Biesenbach).
**Sources:** Biesenbach
