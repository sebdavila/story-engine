# Story types — finding (S060–S065)

## S060 · Big little story
**Purpose:** Turn a small personal event into meaning.
**Choose when:** You have an ordinary event, such as inheriting an object or winning a school contest, that connects to a larger world context and lastingly changed you (Bowles).
**Shape:** Event in scene, the wider context, the shift in you (F060).
**Sources:** Bowles

## S061 · Story Told a Thousand Times
**Purpose:** Find your core themes.
**Choose when:** Friends and family keep asking you to repeat a social story. Mine it for universal themes, then grow it into a stage piece (Leitman).
**Shape:** Take the go-to story, uncover its core universal themes, then develop it into a stage piece (F122).
**Sources:** Leitman

## S062 · Story sources
**Purpose:** Know where stories come from.
**Choose when:** Callahan's sources: Our Stories (first-hand anecdotes noticed in the last 24 hours, turning points, field excursions), Other People's Stories (second-hand, gathered by listening, credited), and Stories from Other Sources (books, experiments, films, ads, podcasts). Dolan splits work stories, which prove capability, from non-work stories (childhood, family, hobbies, errands), which show personality, values and fit.
**Shape:** Credit the source of any second-hand story. Gather with F126 or F120.
**Sources:** Callahan; Dolan

## S063 · Three worlds of personal stories
**Purpose:** Brainstorm without missing a source.
**Choose when:** Topic brainstorming or balancing a collection. Worlds: within us (inner life), between us (family, friends, neighbors), around us (community, work, landscape, history) (Apps).
**Shape:** Draw on all three worlds so relational and environmental stories are not overlooked.
**Sources:** Apps

## S064 · Memory and heritage story
**Purpose:** Pass on roots and ease grief. **Audience:** Children or friends.
**Choose when:** Telling true family memories, including of people who died. Share everyday stories from before and after listeners were born, anchored to an annual ritual; add mother-tongue phrases, lullabies and photo albums (Miller).
**Shape:** Share everyday stories of the person from before and after listeners were born, anchor them to an annual ritual, and weave in heritage language and family photos. Gather detail with F135.
**Sources:** Miller

## S065 · Organizational narrative archetypes
**Purpose:** Recognize plots already circulating in a company.
**Choose when:** Salmon lists recurring plots: moral boundaries within commercial competition (everything for sale except honor); nice bears turned fierce warriors; the jazz band (constant improvisation); paths not taken (counterfactuals); successes into failures and the reverse; what happened told differently (shift a personal experience to a third person to discuss it safely); role-reversal intrigue; success despite organized anarchy; petty bosses versus charismatic managers; hostile-tribe turf battles; the Marseille joke (each teller tops the last).
**Shape:** Use the third-person shift to discuss a sensitive personal experience safely.
**Sources:** Salmon
