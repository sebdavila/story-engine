# Story types — finding (S060–S065)

## S060 · Big little story
**Purpose:** Turn a small personal event into meaning. **Audience:** Listeners at talks or open stages. **Teller:** Individual speaker.
**Choose when:** You have an ordinary event, such as inheriting an object or winning a school contest, that connects to a larger world context and lastingly changed you (Bowles).
**Shape:** Event in scene, the wider context, the shift in you (F060).
**Sources:** Bowles

## S061 · Story Told a Thousand Times
**Purpose:** Find your core themes. **Audience:** Stage audiences. **Teller:** Performer.
**Choose when:** Friends and family keep asking you to repeat a social story. Mine it for universal themes, then grow it into a stage piece (Leitman).
**Shape:** Note why people ask for it; list the recurring themes; develop with F122.
**Sources:** Leitman

## S062 · Story sources
**Purpose:** Know where stories come from. **Audience:** Business audiences. **Teller:** Leader or professional.
**Choose when:** Callahan's sources: Our Stories (first-hand anecdotes noticed in the last 24 hours, turning points, field excursions), Other People's Stories (second-hand, gathered by listening, credited), and Stories from Other Sources (books, experiments, films, ads, podcasts). Dolan splits work stories, which prove capability, from non-work stories (childhood, family, hobbies, errands), which show personality, values and fit. Callahan ranks first-hand highest.
**Shape:** Pick the source by purpose; credit anything second-hand. Gather with F126 or F120.
**Sources:** Callahan; Dolan

## S063 · Three worlds of personal stories
**Purpose:** Brainstorm without missing a source. **Audience:** Readers of memoir or personal essays. **Teller:** Memoirist.
**Choose when:** Topic brainstorming or balancing a collection. Worlds: within us (inner life), between us (family, friends, neighbors), around us (community, work, landscape, history) (Apps).
**Shape:** List a story from each world; check the collection is not all one. Apps pairs inner struggle with a father's sayings and community work bees.
**Sources:** Apps

## S064 · Memory and heritage story
**Purpose:** Pass on roots and ease grief. **Audience:** Children, family, friends. **Teller:** Parent or relative.
**Choose when:** Telling true family memories, including of people who died. Share everyday stories from before and after listeners were born, anchored to an annual ritual; add mother-tongue phrases, lullabies and photo albums (Miller).
**Shape:** Pick a memory, anchor it to a ritual or photo, include heritage language. Gather detail with F135.
**Sources:** Miller

## S065 · Organizational narrative archetypes
**Purpose:** Recognize plots already circulating in a company. **Audience:** Leaders and consultants. **Teller:** Listener.
**Choose when:** Salmon (after Snowden) lists recurring plots: honor within competition; nice bears turned fierce warriors; the jazz band (constant improvisation); paths not taken (counterfactuals); successes into failures and the reverse; what happened told differently (shift a personal experience to a third person to discuss it safely); role-reversal intrigue; success despite organized anarchy; petty bosses versus charismatic managers; hostile-tribe turf battles; the Marseille joke (each teller tops the last).
**Shape:** Listen, name the archetype, work with it. Use the third-person shift for sensitive material.
**Sources:** Salmon
