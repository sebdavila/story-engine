# Story types — structure (S041–S059)

## S041 · Types of big idea
**Purpose:** Choose where the story's central idea comes from. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** you lack a core idea. Options: a story moral (prescriptive or cautionary truth of a genre), a what-if (novel, simple, relatable premise rich in adversity), a craft twist (new narrative, genre, character or world angle), or a wisdom byte.
**Shape:** pick one of the four types; see F026 for a one-sentence promise.
**Sources:** Emunds

## S042 · Scene types by stake movement
**Purpose:** Classify scenes by what they do to the stakes. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** outlining or diagnosing scenes. Primary: inciting incident, midpoint, all-is-lost, climax, where the stake flips. Secondary: other outline beats, where the stake slides. Tertiary: transition, exposition, backstory. Plus genre convention or trope scenes.
**Shape:** classify each scene by type; see F075 for stake thresholds.
**Sources:** Emunds

## S043 · Story length categories
**Purpose:** Size the work. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** planning scope. Epic novel 120,000+ words; novel 50,000 to 100,000; novella 15,000 to 40,000; novelette 10,000 to 20,000; short story 1,000 to 10,000; flash 500 to 1,000; micro 5 to 500.
**Shape:** pick the band. Emunds' ranges overlap, so treat them as guides.
**Sources:** Emunds

## S044 · Action story
**Purpose:** Overcome lethal outer forces and give life meaning through survival. **Audience:** thrill seekers. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** the hero must survive. Need: survival. Value: death/life. Emotion: excitement. Core event: hero at the villain's mercy.
**Shape:** use F077 to tie need, value, emotion and event; build to the hero-at-the-mercy climax.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S045 · War story
**Purpose:** Defend a group's survival while keeping humanity and testing whether a cause justifies dying. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: safety. Value: dishonor/honor (and victory/defeat). Emotion: intrigue. Core event: the climactic battle. Subgenres: pro-war, anti-war, kinship of war.
**Shape:** F077, ending in the big battle.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S046 · Horror story
**Purpose:** Face a monster embodying deep fears, affirming courage and selflessness against a fate worse than death. **Audience:** fear seekers. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: safety. Value: damnation/life. Emotion: fear. Core event: victim at the monster's mercy. Monster subgenres: uncanny (explainable), supernatural, ambiguous.
**Shape:** F077, with the victim at the mercy of the monster at the climax.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S047 · Crime story
**Purpose:** Have a luminary agent unmask a lawbreaker to restore order. **Audience:** puzzle lovers. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: safety. Value: injustice/justice. Emotion: intrigue. Core event: exposure of the criminal. Subgenres by protagonist, setting or crime: murder mystery, hardboiled, procedural, courtroom, heist, espionage.
**Shape:** F077, with exposure of the criminal at the climax.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S048 · Thriller story
**Purpose:** Blend action, horror and crime around a hero tormented by realistic, personal evil. **Audience:** thrill seekers. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: safety. Value: damnation/life. Emotion: excitement. Core event: hero at the villain's mercy. Subgenres: political, serial killer, psychological.
**Shape:** F077; mix S044, S046 and S047 beats.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S049 · Western story
**Purpose:** Probe whether a self-governing outsider endangers order or shields the powerless from tyranny. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: individual sovereignty. Value: subjugation/freedom. Emotion: intrigue. Core event: the big showdown. Subgenres: vengeance, transition, professional.
**Shape:** F077; blends action, crime and society; end at the showdown.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S050 · Love story
**Purpose:** Risk vulnerability and drop selfish desire to reach intimacy. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: connection. Value: hate/love (also indifference, hate disguised as love). Emotion: romance. Core event: proof of love. Subgenres: obsession (desire), courtship (commitment), marriage (intimacy).
**Shape:** F077, ending in proof of love.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S051 · Performance story
**Purpose:** Endure hardship and risk shame to express a gift. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: esteem. Value: shame/respect. Emotion: triumph. Core event: the big performance. Subgenres: business, sports, visual arts, and music and dance.
**Shape:** F077, ending in the big performance.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S052 · Society story
**Purpose:** Examine power between the ruled and the rulers, and whether oppressed leaders resist or are co-opted. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: recognition. Value: impotence/power. Emotion: intrigue, triumph or righteous indignation. Core event: revolution. Subgenres: domestic, women's, biographical, historical, political.
**Shape:** F077, ending in revolution.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S053 · Status story
**Purpose:** Contrast climbing external rank with staying true to inner values. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: respect. Value: failure/success (extending to selling out). Emotion: admiration or pity. Core event: the big choice. Subgenres: pathetic (weak, fails), tragic (hard-working, fatal errors), sentimental (weak, succeeds), admiration (principled, refuses compromise, succeeds).
**Shape:** F077 with F080 (rank versus growth dilemma); end at the big choice.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S054 · Morality story
**Purpose:** Test whether a gifted protagonist acts selfishly or for others. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: self-transcendence. Value: selfishness/altruism. Emotion: satisfaction or contempt. Core event: the big choice. Subgenres: punitive (selfish villain-protagonist punished), redemptive (lost protagonist reclaims the gift for others), testing (protagonist wavers before surrendering to purpose or weakness).
**Shape:** F077, ending at the big choice.
**Sources:** Coyne

## S055 · Worldview story
**Purpose:** Show new reality shattering old beliefs, forcing wisdom or regression. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** need: self-actualization. Value: ignorance/wisdom. Emotion: satisfaction or pity. Core event: cognitive growth or degeneration. Subgenres: disillusionment (faith lost), education (meaninglessness to meaning), maturation (black and white to gray), revelation (vital missing information found).
**Shape:** F077 with F078 (worldview break drives catharsis).
**Sources:** Coyne

## S056 · Two moral formulas: power versus power, or principle versus power
**Purpose:** Separate stories where a strong good side beats a strong bad side from rarer stories where naked principle faces worldly power. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author or reviewer.
**Choose when:** checking whether the story is a power fantasy or a sacrificial truth. Tree of knowledge: principled power against unprincipled power, with victory by force cheered on. Tree of life: the hero loses wealth, status and weapons, and only principle or faithful relationship remains.
**Shape:** decide which formula the story follows. The first stays popular because audiences project onto the winner. See F083.
**Sources:** Schwabauer; Sweet

## S057 · Three resolution plots
**Purpose:** Classify a story by how it resolves, and promise that resolution in the opening. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** choosing the engine. Boy meets girl: a relationship completes a lonely person. Man who learned better: a character faces a flaw and repents. Little tailor (monomyth): a humble hero gains an outer goal through trials, surrendering a false identity for a sacrificial true one.
**Shape:** match opening to plot: a lonely night clerk who keeps almost greeting a regular promises the first. Plots may overlap; classify by resolution, not events. For the third see F005.
**Sources:** Schwabauer; Sweet

## S058 · Shaped story versus plain account
**Purpose:** Add shape and meaning to a plain report of events. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** any.
**Choose when:** a draft reads as a chronology. The plain version (anecdote, or transparent narrative) lists character, predicament and escape, or events in order, and has no persuasive pull. A shaped narrative adds voice, setting and a moral conflict, and shows what it meant.
**Shape:** add a character in moral conflict and the meaning of the events; compare F002.
**Sources:** Schwabauer; Sweet; Gottschall

## S059 · Flawless model hero
**Purpose:** Hold up an ideal against the audience's known flaws. **Audience:** any. **Teller:** author.
**Choose when:** rarely; the aim is to show an aspiration rather than a growth arc.
**Shape:** protagonist without a flaw acts as a yardstick; the audience measures itself against them.
**Sources:** Schwabauer; Sweet
