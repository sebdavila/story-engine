# Questions

Mining, testing and diagnostic questions for business stories. Ask them one at a time and push every general answer back to a single event with a when, a where and a who (F16; T017). In conversation, use them as openers rather than an interview script (T018; F17).

## Personal memory

- **Q001** When did you last feel fully yourself at work, doing exactly what you wanted? — an authentic scene that shows who you are (Edwards)
- **Q002** Tell me about a moment when you knew you had done something exactly right. — a moment of certainty that grounds a credibility story (Livesay)
- **Q003** What did that experience teach you that you still use? — the business takeaway hidden inside a personal episode (Livesay; Dolan)
- **Q004** What made you choose this profession, and what did you do before? — a one-line origin that makes a team introduction memorable (Livesay)
- **Q005** Which three events shaped the person you are today? — the raw list for a who-I-am story (S07) (Callahan)
- **Q006** What is the hardest choice you have ever had to make? — character revealed under pressure (Callahan; Dolan)
- **Q007** When were you forced to pick between two values, two loyalties, or work and family? — tension stories with a real dilemma (Dolan)
- **Q008** When did you make the wrong call, or fail to act for lack of nerve, and still regret it? — tragedy stories that buy trust through honesty (Dolan; Simmons)
- **Q009** Which job moves did you make, why did you leave each one, and which ones were forced on you? — transition stories (Dolan)
- **Q010** In each role, what success or award are you proudest of, and which person or task did you struggle with and work through? — triumph stories with effort behind them (Dolan)
- **Q011** Which mentor, teacher, boss or relative changed who you are, and how? — a signature story with a second character (Forman)
- **Q012** What do you love doing outside work, when did it define you, and how does it link back to your job? — a hobby story that bridges to work values (Forman)
- **Q013** What themes keep repeating in your life, and what repeated wins or frustrations brought you here? — pattern stories (Simmons)
- **Q014** Which good or bad result of your past efforts shaped how you work with people today? — consequence stories (Simmons)
- **Q015** When did you last cry, celebrate wildly, or want to hide under the table? — vulnerability stories (Simmons)
- **Q016** What were your firsts: first job, first sale, first heartbreak? — a cheap, rich vein of specific moments (Hall)
- **Q017** When have you had to be resourceful just to get through? — survival stories (Hall)
- **Q018** What was your most embarrassing moment? — self-deprecating openers (T090) (Hall)
- **Q019** When did someone tell you that you could not do something? — an underdog arc with a clear antagonist (Hall)
- **Q020** What did you live through recently: a struggle, a realization, someone who helped, something you are grateful for? — fresh, small stories for everyday use (Guptara)
- **Q021** When did you prove you are honest, persistent or reliable in one specific incident? — evidence for a trait you want to claim (T027) (Guptara)
- **Q022** What is unusual about you or your background, and what experiences made you who you are? — differentiators for a personal brand story (F42) (Guptara)
- **Q023** What gets you out of bed, or what has frustrated you most (its opposite is your motive)? — the motivation behind a why-I-am-here story (S08) (Edwards)
- **Q024** What story do you tell yourself about why you fail, and whose definition of success are you living? — limiting self-narratives (S14) (Guptara)
- **Q025** What stood out today, did you hear or read a good story, and what point could it make later? — a daily habit that keeps the story bank full (T019; F31) (Callahan)

## Founder

- **Q026** When did you first fall for this craft, as a child or early in life? — the seed of a founder story (S16) (Hall)
- **Q027** When did you first think there has to be a better way, and who was there, what did you feel, what happened next? — the founding moment told as a scene (Hall)
- **Q028** When did everything go wrong in the early days, and what struggle led to the first win? — adversity investors want to see survived (Hall; Edwards)
- **Q029** Which customer need did the company meet first, who spotted it, and how was the answer different from rivals? — the origin story's core (Edwards)
- **Q030** Which early truths still guide the company when it is at its best? — the bridge from origin to present values (Edwards)
- **Q031** Why do you want to succeed, and why at this in particular? — the personal stake behind the venture (Guptara)
- **Q032** What world are you building, and would a listener want to live in it? — the test an investor applies to a pitch (F14) (Livesay)
- **Q033** Why are you the one to do this, and why is now the moment? — qualification and timing proof (Livesay)
- **Q034** Can this founder survive adversity, is the founder fully committed, and do they care? — the unspoken judgments a founder story must answer (F19) (Hall)
- **Q035** What was the worst day in the history of the business? — a low point that makes the climb believable (Hall)
- **Q036** Which single event or decision could the company not have survived without? — the pivotal scene of the company's history (Hall)
- **Q037** When did you see the old strategy would stop working, why did you hold it, and how did you find out? — a strategy pivot story (F08) (Guptara)
- **Q038** What is the ultimate goal of the work, and which project shows it best? — a mission story with a concrete case (Edwards)
- **Q039** Who do we become if this goes right, what do customers gain, and what are the risks of acting and of not acting? — a vision story checked against reality (S19) (Forman)

## Customer

- **Q040** What problem keeps your customer awake, how does it feel, and how does it hurt their life and business? — the Normal beat of a value story (F01) (Hall)
- **Q041** How does using your product change their day, and what does that feel like? — the turn of a value story (Hall)
- **Q042** After the change, what is better, how do they feel, and which pains are gone? — the after beat (Hall)
- **Q043** If you could not mention or show the product, what would you tell customers? — the benefit hidden behind features (T024) (Hall)
- **Q044** What were you using before, and what made you willing to try us? — the switching moment of a customer story (S09; F18) (Hall)
- **Q045** Where were you when you first tried the product or heard of it? — a time and place to open the customer story (T002) (Hall)
- **Q046** Whose life is different because your business exists? — candidates for a customer hero (F21) (Hall)
- **Q047** When did you make a customer cry, for better or worse, and when did you stop one from crying? — emotional extremes worth telling (Hall)
- **Q048** Who is your happiest customer, and who is your unhappiest? — success stories and honest lessons (Hall)
- **Q049** What was your first sale, your most meaningful sale, and one you lost? — sales stories with real stakes (Hall)
- **Q050** What happened next, and what would have happened if it had failed? — stakes added to a flat case study (Livesay)
- **Q051** What is the best and the worst part of your job? — operational pain under a price objection (Livesay)
- **Q052** What would make your boss happiest? — how to make the buyer the hero inside their company (F36) (Livesay)
- **Q053** One month after the project, what would you need to see to call it a success? — success markers for future pacing (T077) (Livesay)
- **Q054** Walk me step by step through a recent deal: how you found the prospect, first contact, resources used, who decided, how you closed. — what top performers do differently (F26) (McKinsey)
- **Q055** Does anyone have a story from the last few days of something special happening with a customer? — a steady flow of service stories from front-line teams (F40) (Callahan)
- **Q056** How do your customers see themselves, what do they want, and how do they believe they make a difference? — the audience's own story to align with (T079) (Edwards)
- **Q057** What do clients only appreciate once they are clients, and which part of your process must they understand to get it? — the hidden way that differentiates you (Guptara)
- **Q058** Can the customer recognize themselves in the story? — fit between the hero and the buyer (F22) (Parikh)

## Objections

- **Q059** Why do people not choose you, and what do prospects ask most often? — the objections a story must defuse (S11) (Hall)
- **Q060** What do people object to about your decision, you or your company, without saying it? — material for a pre-empt story (Guptara)
- **Q061** Who are you, why are you here, and what do you gain from my cooperation? — the three silent questions a listener needs answered first (F19) (Simmons)
- **Q062** Do we need this, are you the best one to deliver it, and can we work with you? — the buyer's private checklist in a pitch (Callahan)
- **Q063** What story are listeners already telling themselves about you or this issue, and what decent intention drives their resistance? — the competing narrative to answer (F33) (Simmons)
- **Q064** When did this last happen, where, who was there, and what came next? — a complaint moved from generality back to an event (F17) (Simmons)
- **Q065** What experience made you feel this way, or which stories convinced you this option is bad? — the root of a hard objection (Simmons)
- **Q066** Let me check I heard you: is your question this? Did my answer cover it fully? — confirmed listening in Q&A (T065) (Livesay)
- **Q067** Does that make sense, and where should we go from here? — a collaborative close to an objection (F35) (Livesay)
- **Q068** What else would you need to know to feel comfortable hiring us, and if the others agree, can we count on you? — final concerns surfaced before the close (Livesay)
- **Q069** It sounds like you are upset about A, B and C; did I miss anything? — every grievance on the table when a client is angry (T083) (Livesay)
- **Q070** Which risks and flaws in our own proposal should we name before they do? — objections voiced with workarounds (T073) (Edwards)
- **Q071** What might make people resist this future: bad past experience, competing interests, low morale, inertia? — sources of pushback to plan for (Forman)
- **Q072** From the employee's side, what does this change mean for me, and why should I care about one more initiative? — resistance to an internal change (Dolan)
- **Q073** What does the action I am asking for mean inside their current story? — whether the request sounds like pride or another corporate fad (Simmons)

## Leadership lesson

- **Q074** What personal experience most convinced you of what you now want others to believe? — a story that can recreate the same shift in listeners (F32) (Simmons)
- **Q075** When did you first learn this lesson or find this truth? — the origin of the point you want to make (S12) (Hall)
- **Q076** When did you swear you would never do that again, and what is the most important lesson you have learned? — lesson stories with a cost attached (Callahan; Simmons)
- **Q077** What was your biggest mistake or career turning point, and what would you do differently? — honest teaching stories (Simmons)
- **Q078** When did you put people before results? — a caring story (Callahan)
- **Q079** When were you surprised by what you truly care about? — values discovered in action (Callahan)
- **Q080** When was your purpose clearest, and when did a group exceed expectations because it believed in the work? — purpose stories (S17) (Callahan)
- **Q081** When did you walk away because your values and the organization's no longer matched? — a values line drawn in public (Callahan)
- **Q082** When did you risk your reputation for a bigger cause? — a courage story (Callahan)
- **Q083** When did a small innovation make a big difference? — an innovation story leaders can repeat (Callahan)
- **Q084** When did a team you were in achieve something against the odds, and what happened? — a collaboration story (Callahan; Edwards)
- **Q085** When did you see people come from behind or turn a bad situation around, and which moments do you revisit for inspiration? — inspiration stories (Callahan)
- **Q086** How many stories can you tell that prove each value you claim? — whether values are lived or only printed (S18) (Simmons)
- **Q087** What does each company value mean, who embodies it, and when did the company give up a short-term gain to keep it? — values stories with a fork in the road (Edwards)
- **Q088** When did the organization have to change, what failed first, what tipped it, and who championed it? — a change story (Edwards)
- **Q089** When did the organization beat a daunting obstacle, how did people feel, and what did it learn? — an overcoming-challenges story (Edwards)
- **Q090** When did you see our purpose in action? — evidence for a purpose story (Hall)
- **Q091** Do people know how the company started, its setbacks, its biggest win and its comebacks? — gaps in the shared culture story (F40) (Hall)
- **Q092** Why did you join, why here rather than elsewhere, and what proud moment shows what we stand for? — ownership stories for divided teams (Guptara; Dolan)
- **Q093** What makes you proud to work here, and how has the company helped a person or community? — employee stories for the brand (Forman)
- **Q094** Which story best explains the decision or change we are making? — the single clarity story for a leadership team (Callahan)
- **Q095** What does this story mean for you? Does it remind you of anything? Could it happen here, and what would you need to start? — listeners turn a story into their own action (F41) (Callahan)
- **Q096** How is this situation like what happens at work? — an analogy that carries the lesson (S13) (Callahan)
- **Q097** Which conclusions are you imposing instead of letting the team connect the dots? — room for co-creation (T076) (Guptara)
- **Q098** Picture the future you hope for, or the one you fear, as a scene with named people: what happens to them? — a future story for a vision or a warning (S19) (Simmons)

## Data

- **Q099** What do these numbers mean, which narrative do they support, and how would this audience best take it in? — the move from analysis to narrative (F24) (Parikh)
- **Q100** Which single fact holds the most value for this audience, and what can be cut? — the one insight (F23) (Edwards)
- **Q101** What is going on and how did we get here? — the situation an audience already accepts (F04) (McKinsey)
- **Q102** What were your critical assumptions, how sure are you, and what are the upside and downside risks? — credibility for the complication (McKinsey)
- **Q103** How can the organization get out of this, and are there bolder moves that turn smaller losses into profit? — the step from conservative to radical actions (McKinsey)
- **Q104** How should we respond to the projected trend, and can anything reverse it? — implications of a forecast (McKinsey)
- **Q105** What impact does this problem have, and is it within the decision-maker's authority? Repeat until you pass their remit, then step back one level. — the right scope for the problem (F11) (McKinsey)
- **Q106** Do we know enough about the problem to address the real need? — premature answers (Parikh)
- **Q107** These are the areas we considered: do you agree, what is missing, which matter most? — co-created problem framing (Parikh)
- **Q108** Is the starting point an observable fact or a firm point of agreement? — a safe opening for a logical chain (F10) (Parikh)
- **Q109** Can each premise be proven, and is the case built on three to five distinct reasons that match the audience's criteria? — gaps in the argument (Parikh)
- **Q110** Should the recommendation come first or last? — the reveal order for this room (T009) (Parikh)
- **Q111** Can the audience decode the chart in under ten seconds, and what would they take from its title alone? — charts that carry the message (T029; F25) (Parikh)
- **Q112** Does the body of each slide prove the claim in its title, and can it be covered in three minutes? — weak or overloaded slides (McKinsey)
- **Q113** If only the slide titles remained, could a reader follow the story? — the narrative spine (T029) (Parikh)
- **Q114** Which supporting data, credible champion, sequence of support and mix of channels will travel with the story? — the evidence plan for a strategic initiative (Forman)

## Diagnose a draft

- **Q115** What should the audience do, think and feel after this, and where are they now on the topic? — the brief the draft must serve (T022; F46) (Edwards; Livesay; Hall)
- **Q116** Who is the audience, what matters to them, and what value can you offer? — audience fit (F22) (Edwards; Parikh)
- **Q117** What purpose, with whom, and why now; and who should tell it? — timing and teller (T023) (Forman)
- **Q118** Which key stakeholders can help or block the outcome, and what are their interests and likely concerns? — who the story must win (Parikh; Forman)
- **Q119** Why are you telling this story, what does it prove, and how does it connect to the business situation now? — a story without a job (T007) (Dolan)
- **Q120** What is the main point, in one sentence? — the takeaway to land after the story (T008) (Callahan)
- **Q121** Who is the hero, what do they want, and who or what stands in the way? — missing character or conflict (F02; F34) (Maxwell)
- **Q122** Is it their story or yours, slide by slide and paragraph by paragraph? — teller-centered drafts (Edwards)
- **Q123** This is the slide where they… — if you cannot finish the sentence, cut or rework the part (Edwards)
- **Q124** Where have you taken them, what are they thinking now, and what will they ask next? — the inner monologue the next beat must answer (T030) (Edwards; McKinsey)
- **Q125** Is it clear, does it carry an important point, does it fit in 30 to 60 seconds, and does it move feelings or understanding? — whether the story earns its place (Livesay)
- **Q126** Why did I tell it, who was it about, what problem did the hero face, what did they learn, and what changed? — missing story elements (F02) (Maxwell)
- **Q127** If it captivates but misses the goal, is the character right and does the turn match the objective? — misaligned stories (Hall)
- **Q128** Is every detail and number in the middle directly relevant to the point? — clutter (T028) (Dolan)
- **Q129** Where is the sensory detail that lets listeners see, hear and feel the scene? — abstract telling (T101; T102) (Forman)
- **Q130** Where is the unexpected moment, and have you removed jargon and corporate-speak? — flat, stilted drafts (T012; T104) (Forman)
- **Q131** Does the opening grab, and does each event lead inevitably to the next while suspense builds? — pacing (T011) (Forman)
- **Q132** Is it too personal or self-indulgent for this audience? — oversharing (S03) (Forman)
- **Q133** What does this story say about me, and what point did you hear? — ask a story buddy to check what lands (Callahan)
- **Q134** Is it compatible with what the audience knows, plausible, verifiable, and believed by others? — credibility risks (T107) (Callahan)
- **Q135** What emotion does each key point need, and which examples will trigger it? — emotional design (T067) (Parikh; Edwards)
- **Q136** How do you feel about this meeting, how is the audience feeling, and what emotional journey must you guide them through? — emotional needs of the room (F30) (Edwards)
- **Q137** Before they ask: what happens next, what must I and my team do, and how will you help? — a missing implementation path (Edwards)
- **Q138** Will people care enough to act: buy, follow, believe, accept the change? — emotional connection to the desired action (Dolan)
- **Q139** Where can I speak more plainly, and where did professionalism become distance? — corporate voice (T104) (Guptara)
- **Q140** Am I selling features or benefits, and am I the hero or the guide? — misplaced hero (F03) (Guptara)
- **Q141** How will length, point of view and language change for each stakeholder group? — one story adapted across audiences (T015) (Forman)
- **Q142** Where are trial listeners interested, and when are they bored, confused or resistant? — weak spots found in rehearsal (T058; T059) (Forman)
- **Q143** Is this boring? — asked aloud, it turns a restless room back toward its own interests (T061) (Simmons)
- **Q144** If no story makes your goal feel important to others, is the goal important? — whether the outcome itself is the problem (Simmons)
